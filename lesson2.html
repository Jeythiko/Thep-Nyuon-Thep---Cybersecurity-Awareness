Protect Yourself Online

Phishing and Malacious Links

This section will clue you up on how fake messages, emails and websites can be used to steal information.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of cyberattack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person, company, or service in order to trick you into giving away sensitive information or performing an action that benefits the attacker. The attacker might try to steal:

Personal Information Financial Data Login Credentials Verification codes (OTP) Banking or payment information Access to your computer or account Passwords Email/account credentials

So when you recieve a suspicious email, message or website link, be cautious and verify it, your privacy matters the most and that's the purpose of this website.

Example

Imagine you receive an email that appears to come from your bank:

"Your account has been temporarily locked. Click here to verify your account."

Instead of the real bank website, you're taken to a fake website that looks almost identical.

Username: Gatluak Password: ********

When you send your information, you're potentially giving away sensitive data that can be used against you such that the attacker will trick you to pay to release your information or you will eventually lose your account.

What is Malicious Link?

A malicious link is a link designed to lead you to something harmful or deceptive, such as a fake login page, malware download, scam website, or another dangerous destination.

For example, a message might say:

Your account has been suspended or I saw your pictures on this website. Click this link!

At this point when you rush into clicking the link, the website will completely look different from the website you expect, and boom! your account is gone, you lost your sensitive information

Therefore keep yourself composed and never make that mistake of giving your sensitive information away.

Example

Imagine you receive:

Your Facebook account will be permanently locked! Verify now.

The link appears as:

facebook.com/verify

But when you hover over it, the actual destination is:

https://facebook-security.example.net/login

Bro, give it a red flag, that nigga tryna scam or hack you.

How to Protect Yourself 1. Don't click suspicious links

If you receive a message from your bank, for example, open the bank's official app or type the known website yourself instead of clicking the message link.

2. Check the sender

Always check the sender's email address or phone number before responding to any request for information.

3. Be cautious with personal information

Never share sensitive information like passwords, PINs, or account details unless you are certain of the recipient's identity.

4. Use MFA/2FA

Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) or two-factor authentication (2FA) on your accounts to add an extra layer of security.

5. Never give someone your OTP

Attacker might try to trick you into revealing your one-time password (OTP). When you recieve the OTP, check the source before revealing it.

6. Slow down

Take your time when dealing with unexpected messages or requests for information. Rushing can lead to mistakes.

7. Keep your devices updated

Ensure your devices and software are up to date with the latest security patches.
